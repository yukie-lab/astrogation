Keywords: warp drive, general relativity, energy conditions, photon rocket, Bondi–Sachs momentum

Abstract
A useful warp drive must change its velocity, yet known positive-energy constructions are
static or constant-velocity, while accelerating ones require exotic matter; as Bobrick and
Martire noted, warp drives “do not have any natural way of changing their velocities.” We
first settle the question of principle by reading the Bondi–Sachs balance as a propulsion
law: any asymptotically flat drive with a confined, dominant-energy material source (and
standard peeling) changes its Bondi four-momentum only by radiating to null infinity, so it
cannot steer without radiating, the general-relativistic counterpart of the relativistic
rocket. We then exhibit a positive-energy spacetime that saturates it. Prescribing the
passenger worldtube and accepting positive null radiation as the exterior (the exact
Kinnersley photon rocket), we solve for the matching shell: the mechanism is
photon-rocket recoil on a tidally protected flat cavity, and the decoupled flat interior, not a
warp field, makes it a warp drive. The exterior energy conditions reduce to n2 ≥ 0, and
steering obeys the closed-form control law −ṁ ≥ 3m|a|, paid by Bondi mass loss. The
static matched shell satisfies the surface dominant energy condition for 2m/R < 24/25,
and admissible accelerating shells exist unconditionally at small acceleration about it,
below a rigorous closed-form acceleration–compactness ceiling. The least-radiating
maneuver is the gravitational-wave–silent Damour dipole, and minimum-radiation steering
is a hyperbolic geodesic with the universal Tsiolkovsky constant 3. A slow fixed-axis burn
assembles, perturbatively, into a time-evolved spacetime. The drive is causal, subluminal,
and a reaction drive: steering costs an order-unity fraction of the rest mass for mildly
relativistic boosts, but needs no exotic matter. Steering a warp drive is a problem of
energy budget, not negative energy.
1 Introduction
Alcubierre’s warp drive [1] showed that general relativity permits a compact region (a “bubble”) to
be transported through an ambient spacetime while its interior remains locally flat. The price was
exotic matter: the construction violates the null energy condition (nec), and with it the weak,
strong, and dominant conditions. This is not an accident of Alcubierre’s particular ansatz.
Santiago, Schuster, and Visser [2] proved that generic warp drives, defined through the standard
shift-vector ansatz, violate the nec; Lobo and Visser [3] had earlier quantified the unavoidable
exotic content, and Pfenning and Ford [4] showed that the required negative energy is further
constrained by quantum inequalities [5].
Two design philosophies dominate the field, and both are overconstrained. Metric-first
designs [1, 6, 7] choose the geometry and let Einstein’s equations dictate the matter, which then
turns out to be exotic. Source-first or static designs [8, 9, 10, 11] prescribe a physically reasonable
matter profile and tolerate the geometry that results; these reach positive energy or reduced exotic
content, but are static or constant-velocity. The positive-energy claims of Lentz [12] and
Fell–Heisenberg [13] take a different route, but their energy-condition status remains disputed [14]
(Table 1). Bobrick and Martire state the gap plainly: warp drives “do not have any natural way of
changing their velocities,” and “no metric which describes an accelerating warp drive solution has
so far been presented in the literature” [8]. Barzegar, Buchert, and Vigneron [15] (see also [16])
raise a complementary structural requirement: a physical warp drive must possess covariant (not
merely coordinate) velocity and acceleration, and a genuine mechanism derived from matter rather
than reverse-engineered from a chosen metric. Buchert and Frackowiak [17] report an instability of
warp fields in that metric-first class (Sec. 14).

dominant energy condition, in bulk and shell and over all observers, together with a covariant,
matter-derived acceleration (Table 1, Sec. 14).
In a companion paper [18] we tested whether the source-first route can be pushed to
admissibility. Using the observer-robust energy-condition framework of Ref. [19], an exhaustive
scan of compact-support shell configurations found none admissible: the dominant-energy failures
localize at the smooth source–vacuum transition, persist in the static limit, and appear as
Hawking–Ellis [20] Type IV stress-energy in the transition tail. The obstruction is geometric, tied
to the transition itself rather than to the warp velocity, and we re-derive this boundary cost in
closed form below (Prop. 7). That result reframes the problem: if the obstruction is the transition
itself, the design should remove the smooth source–vacuum transition from its center and abandon
the requirement that the exterior be vacuum.
This paper. We propose a third philosophy, worldtube-first. We prescribe the passenger
worldtube (a timelike curve Γ together with a regular cavity) and we accept positive null radiation
as the exterior matter we are willing to carry; Einstein’s equations then determine the shell. The
exterior is taken to be the exact Kinnersley–Robinson–Trautman photon rocket [21, 22, 23, 24, 25],
a known exact solution whose source is pure outgoing null dust. Its energy conditions reduce to a
single positivity, n2 ≥ 0 (Sec. 5), and its central worldline is covariantly accelerated, by the recoil of
anisotropic radiation, exactly as a photon rocket. We excise the worldline singularity, replace
r < R(u, Ω) by a regular cavity, and match across a timelike worldtube using the standard Lanczos
junction together with flux-source balance laws [26, 27]: the timelike-shell balance laws with an
external crossing flux, mandatory here because the exterior radiation is emitted at the shell, the
cavity within being vacuum.
The resulting object, a radiative momentum warpshell, is causal, subluminal, self-gravitating,
and built entirely from matter that satisfies the energy conditions in the bulk. Its acceleration is
real and is paid for by Bondi mass loss. The organizing result is not the metric but a conservation
law : no asymptotically flat, positive-energy drive can change its velocity without radiating
four-momentum to null infinity, and the construction below is the explicit positive-energy object
that saturates it. This makes rigorous, as a Bondi–Sachs conservation law, the momentum-balance
obstruction Bobrick and Martire could state only heuristically [8]: because warp spacetimes are
asymptotically flat, a confined drive acquires four-momentum only by radiating it, and we exhibit
the positive-energy object that pays exactly that price with n2 ≥ 0. We summarize the
contributions:
1. A model-independent no-reactionless-steering principle, the capstone (Sec. 3,
Theorem 1): for any asymptotically flat, dominant-energy drive with a confined material
source (and Bondi–Sachs peeling), the Bondi four-momentum changes only through radiation
to null infinity, so steering forces radiation. The balance follows directly from the curvature
across three independent exact spacetimes. The contributions below are its faces in an
explicit positive-energy instance that saturates it.
2. Bulk energy conditions for the exterior collapse to the single scalar inequality n2 ≥ 0
(Sec. 5).
3. A control law −ṁ ≥ 3m|a|, the local dipole face of the principle (Corollary 3), ties covariant
proper acceleration to Bondi mass loss, with ma the Bondi momentum flux, the thrust
(Sec. 6).
4. An unconditional small-amplitude existence theorem: admissible accelerating shells
exist about the static Schwarzschild–Minkowski anchor, whose surface dominant energy
condition holds strictly for 2m/R < 24/25. Exact matching reduces to a single closed-form
scalar D(x) (58), bounded away from zero (|D| > 43 ) across the window, so an admissible
accelerating shell exists for every small a > 0 (Theorem 2, App. D), with matched surface
stress of Hawking–Ellis Type I and a strictly positive observer-robust surface-dec margin.

2 What is a warp drive? A worldtube-first definition
The recurring criticism of warp spacetimes is that their “motion” is a coordinate statement [15, 16]:
a shift vector β i (t, x) in an ADM form ds2 = −dt2 + γij (dxi + β i dt)(dxj + β j dt) encodes a
coordinate velocity that need not correspond to any covariant acceleration of matter. We therefore
adopt an operational, gauge-invariant definition.
Definition 1 (Warpshell). A warpshell is a spacetime M = M− ∪Σ M+ such that: (i) the interior
M− is a regular timelike worldtube (the cavity) with bounded tidal tensor along a central
worldline Γ; (ii) Σ is a timelike hypersurface across which the induced metric is continuous (the
first junction condition) and the surface stress-energy Sab is well defined; (iii) the exterior M+
satisfies the energy conditions; and (iv) the central worldline carries a prescribed covariant proper
√
acceleration aµ = uν ∇ν uµ with gauge-invariant magnitude aµ aµ . The warpshell is admissible if,
in addition, Sab satisfies the surface energy conditions.
Definition 1 makes “acceleration” a scalar invariant of a physical worldline, not a coordinate
velocity, answering the covariance demand of Refs. [15, 16]. It also inverts the design order
demanded there: we will fix Γ and the admissible exterior matter first, and let the Einstein
equations return Sab . Alcubierre and Natário bubbles are warpshells in this sense only in a
degenerate, non-accelerating limit; the constant-velocity positive-energy shells of Refs. [8, 9] realize
(i)–(iii) but reach (iv) only in the non-accelerating limit aµ = 0. Our task is to satisfy all four with
the exterior energy conditions intact.
In what sense a warp drive. The object we construct is, mechanically, a rocket: it expends
mass as radiation, and its passengers feel proper acceleration. What distinguishes it from a bare
rocket, and places it in the warp-drive lineage, is the structure of Definition 1: a passenger cavity
that is exactly flat, so the interior tidal tensor vanishes and the occupants ride in a tidally flat,
accelerated laboratory, joined to an exterior that is positive-energy in every frame. It is the
accelerating, positive-energy member of the family whose constant-velocity members are the shells
of Refs. [8, 9]: what it shares with the Alcubierre program is the protected flat interior, and what it
adds is a covariant, matter-derived change of state of motion. Unlike the Alcubierre bubble it

carries no local expansion or contraction of space ahead of or behind the cavity (no York-time
“warp field”): the cavity is rigidly Rindler-accelerated and the exterior is the photon rocket, so the
warp-drive connection is precisely the decoupled, tidally protected passenger laboratory, not a
spatial-expansion mechanism. We claim no superluminal travel and no reactionless propulsion
(Sec. 15); the contribution is that the acceleration long missing from positive-energy warp drives is
achievable at the price of ordinary radiation.
3 The no-reactionless-steering principle
The organizing principle of the paper is a statement stronger than “this particular construction
radiates”: no confined, positive-energy drive can steer without paying a momentum debt to null
infinity. We state it first, as a model-independent theorem with a self-contained proof skeleton, and
only then build the saturating object: the explicit worldtube-first construction of Sec. 4 below
(whose control law (13) (positivity of the null-dust amplitude, Prop. 3) and momentum balance
(11) we invoke as needed) is its local, positive-energy saturating face. We state the law, identify the
one hypothesis the construction must supply, and record how the Kinnersley photon rocket of
Sec. 4 saturates it.

Confined sources only. The theorem governs the total Bondi four-momentum of a confined
source, and the confinement hypothesis is what gives the no-go its force. A device that expels
unconfined massive reaction mass can change its payload’s velocity while the total Bondi
four-momentum stays fixed, the momentum being carried to timelike infinity i+ by the propellant
rather than to I + ; but that is an ordinary rocket with a material exhaust, not a reactionless drive,
and its propellant leaves every bounded region, violating the hypothesis. For a positive-energy
source that stays confined the only momentum channel to infinity is the null radiation reaching I + ,
so steering forces that radiation.
The remaining hypothesis needing care is the identification of PBi with the kinematic steering of
the passenger worldline.

Relation to the classical Bondi–Sachs balance. The flux-balance law (1) is classical
Bondi–Sachs, and the recoil of a source by anisotropic radiation is older still [47, 48]; we claim no
novelty for it. The content of Theorem 1 is (i) reading it as a propulsion no-go (reactionless
steering is forbidden in general relativity, Corollary 1); (ii) closing the “borrow momentum for free”
loophole with Bondi-mass positivity (Corollary 2); (iii) separating it cleanly from the
center-of-mass swimming effect (Proposition 1); and (iv) exhibiting an explicit, fully positive-energy
spacetime that saturates it. It is the positive-energy complement of Santiago–Schuster–Visser [2]:
they forbid the metric-first warp drive (the prescribed shift-vector ansatz with vacuum exterior,
tested over all observers); the present no-go prices the source-first one and shows the price is
payable with n2 ≥ 0. It supplies the covariant accelerator that Refs. [15, 16] demand (dPBi /du ̸= 0
is a geometric Bondi-frame momentum flux at null infinity), and places the constant-velocity
positive-energy shells [8, 9, 10] as the dPBi /du = 0 boundary case. The Tsiolkovsky budget (14) is
its integrated form, making the construction the general-relativistic counterpart of the relativistic
rocket [49]. Geometrically the law is a moment-map/symplectic statement on the
Ashtekar–Streubel radiative phase space (Sec. 11.1): the asymptotic-translation Hamiltonian is the
radiated four-momentum flux, PBµ the cut charge for the same asymptotic translation (linked by
the balance law dPBµ /du = −F µ ), and the covariant steering is the frame-invariant projected recoil
Rµ (41), which vanishes on the constant-velocity (C4 = 0) face of Prop. 8.

7.4 Reconciliation with the boundary-cost theorem
Reference [18] found no admissible smooth shells, the dominant-energy failure localizing at the
source–vacuum transition and persisting in the static limit, the closed-form radial-tension
obstruction of Prop. 7 below. This does not contradict Theorem 2. The boundary cost is carried by
the radial gradients and induced anisotropy of a finite-width matter profile dying into vacuum; it
appears as Type IV stress-energy in the transition tail. The radiative warpshell removes both
ingredients: the transition is a measure-zero distributional junction (no finite-width gradient tail),
and the exterior is not vacuum: it is positive null radiation, so there is no “matter dies into
vacuum” curvature spike at the place where momentum must be transported. The static anchor of
Sec. 7.1 is the clearest example: the thin Schwarzschild–Minkowski shell satisfies dec strictly for
x < 24/25, exactly the regime the smooth scan could not reach. The thin-shell limit does not hide
the boundary cost; it replaces the obstructing geometry by an admissible one, as Theorem 2
establishes.

10 Gravitational-wave silence
Whereas the preceding section concerned the wall’s local matter realization, the gravitational
radiation reaching null infinity is fixed by the exterior class alone, and it vanishes. The exterior is
left exactly Kinnersley by the excision; the Bondi news N (u, Ω) of the matched spacetime equals

wave-generation theory: for the purely dipolar emission the leading 1/r waves from the accelerated
mass cancel exactly against those of the momentum-carrying radiation field, so no gravitational
radiation reaches null infinity. Hence dipole steering is gravitational-wave silent, and the control
law (13) suffers no uncontrolled radiative correction. Higher-multipole steering reintroduces a news
contribution and an additional energy-loss channel; the minimum-radiation maneuver is therefore
the Damour dipole, which we now make precise.

14.2 Why the Santiago–Schuster–Visser theorem is evaded
Reference [2] proves that generic warp drives violate the nec. Its hypotheses define a warp drive
through the shift-vector ansatz with an asymptotically Minkowski (vacuum) exterior, and the
violation is a property of the stress-energy that the chosen geometry forces. The radiative
warpshell does not satisfy those hypotheses: we do not prescribe the metric, the exterior is not
vacuum, and the matter we install (positive null radiation) satisfies the nec by Proposition 3. The
theorem is not contradicted; its premise (a vacuum shift-bubble) simply does not describe our
construction. Equally important is how positivity is established: a central lesson of Ref. [2] is that
earlier “positive-energy” claims inspected only the comoving Eulerian observer, whereas the energy
conditions quantify over all timelike and null observers; our bulk and shell margins are computed
observer-robustly over the boost manifold (App. K), so the binding test is not concealed by a frame
choice, the very failure mode the theorem exposes. The only place the nec could fail is the shell,
and Theorem 2 establishes that it does not on an open admissible set.
14.3 Structural soundness beyond the energy conditions
Barzegar, Buchert, and collaborators [16, 15] stress that energy-condition violation is not the only
pathology of warp spacetimes: a physical drive must also have a well-defined asymptotic structure
and covariant kinematics, and several cataloged constructions fail on those grounds independently
of any exotic matter. A deeper concern of Refs. [15, 16] is methodological: prescribing a metric and
reading off whatever stress-energy the Einstein equations return can manufacture sources with no
interpretation as physical matter. The worldtube-first construction is not subject to this objection
by design: it does not reverse-engineer the stress-energy from a chosen metric but installs
interpretable matter region by region (real outgoing null dust outside, vacuum cavity,
Hawking–Ellis Type I shell; Sec. 2, Prop. 3), the discipline those references call for. The recent
no-go theorems of Ref. [15] and the new realizations of Buchert and Frackowiak [17] (which also
report instability in that class, consonant with our stability caveat of Sec. 15) bear on the
restricted, metric-first (flow-orthogonal, prescribed-shift) class; the worldtube-first construction,
with its interpretable null-dust exterior and a geometrically defined Bondi four-momentum, lies
outside their hypotheses. The radiative warpshell is built to pass these structural tests. Its velocity

and acceleration are Bondi-frame components of the geometrically defined four-momentum PBµ (u)
of the exact Kinnersley exterior (Sec. 3, Cor. 4; with |a| the covariant scalar of Definition 1(iv)),
not coordinate artifacts. The spacetime is free of closed causal curves and globally hyperbolic on
the finite maneuver slab: the Rindler horizon stays off the cavity for λ = aR < 1, and eternal
acceleration is not claimed (Sec. 15). The drive therefore sidesteps the structural no-go catalog, not
merely the null-energy one.
14.4 Quantum inequalities do not apply
Ford–Roman–type quantum inequalities [5, 4] bound the magnitude and duration of negative
energy densities. The radiative warpshell contains none: bulk and (admissible) shell stress-energy
are positive. The quantum-inequality no-go that rules out Alcubierre and Krasnikov [77] is
therefore inapplicable.

16 Conclusion
We have inverted the warp-drive design problem. Rather than prescribing a metric (and inheriting
exotic matter) or a static source (and forgoing acceleration), we prescribe the passenger worldtube,
accept positive null radiation as the exterior, and solve for the shell. The exterior is an exact
photon rocket whose energy conditions reduce to n2 ≥ 0, steering obeys the positive-energy control
law −ṁ ≥ 3m|a|, and admissible accelerating shells exist unconditionally at small amplitude about
the static Schwarzschild–Minkowski anchor, the ℓ = 1 matching reducing to the single closed-form
scalar D(x). An acceleration–compactness frontier caps the proper acceleration, below the rigorous
closed-form ceiling 21 (1 − x).
The organizing result is model-independent: no asymptotically flat, dominant-energy drive with
a confined material source can change its velocity without radiating four-momentum to null infinity
(Theorem 1). This flux balance is classical Bondi–Sachs; our contribution is to read it as a
no-reactionless-steering principle, the general-relativistic counterpart of the relativistic rocket, and
to read it directly from the curvature across the Kinnersley rocket, static Schwarzschild, and
Vaidya. A confined positive-energy system cannot change its Bondi four-momentum without
radiating, so reactionless steering is impossible; the velocity reading is exact between stationary
Bondi frames and, for the closed-form Kinnersley rocket, instantaneously (Prop. 1). Our
construction is the explicit positive-energy saturating instance of this law for an accelerating warp
drive, with the minimum-radiation maneuver the gravitational-wave-silent Damour dipole.
Acceleration is paid for by positive radiation, not negative energy. Within the compactness window
x + 2aR < 4/5 the thin shell is realized as the limit of an admissible radiating tangential-pressure
wall that stays dominant-energy-admissible under its own O(µ) back-reaction, and the worked
saturating burn (App. K) lies inside this window throughout, so that burn itself, not merely its
endpoints, is realized by an admissible radiating wall at the adiabatic level (Secs. 9.2, 9.3).
Several results are fully analytic; we record them as the rigorous results:
• the closed-form control law and the bifurcation scalar D(x) (Lemma 2, App. D);
• the warp-drive identity as a diffeomorphism-invariant charge classification (Prop. 8);
• the radiating-wall back-reaction sign (Theorem 6, Prop. 10);
• the radial-tension thickening obstruction with its 1/δ 2 boundary-cost law (Prop. 7, Cor. 5);
• the gravitational-wave silence of the Damour dipole, analytic from the exact Kinnersley news,
with a numerical peeling cross-check (App. I);
• maneuver design as a state-constrained geodesic-control theorem on H3 with universal
Tsiolkovsky constant 3 (Theorems 7, 5).
The construction extends to Λ > 0 (Sec. 12).

We analyze the radial stability of the static Schwarzschild–Minkowski shell by the Poisson–Visser

Whether a stress-energy satisfies the dominant energy condition is decided not by the field
equations but by a pointwise optimization over all observers, which for an anisotropic, accelerated
shell has no closed form; deciding admissibility is therefore an irreducibly computational
complement to the analytic development of the main text, carried out observer-robustly [19, 84]
