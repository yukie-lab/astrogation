Keywords: warp drive, general relativity, energy conditions, photon rocket, Bondi–Sachs momentum

Abstract
A useful warp drive must accelerate, yet every compact positive-energy construction is
static or constant-velocity; accelerating ones need exotic matter, abandon the dominant
energy condition (DEC), or are non-compact (no Bondi four-momentum to balance). We
construct a compact positive-energy object driven by photon-rocket recoil on a tidally
shielded flat cavity. The principle is Bondi–Sachs balance read as propulsion: any
asymptotically flat drive with a confined dominant-energy source changes its Bondi
four-momentum only by radiating to null infinity, so it cannot steer without radiating. A
Kinnersley photon-rocket exterior with prescribed passenger worldtube fixes the matching
thin shell, whose flat interior makes it a warp drive. Bulk energy conditions collapse to
n2 ≥ 0; steering obeys the closed-form law −ṁ ≥ 3m|a|, paid by Bondi mass loss, and
both budget and kinematic ceiling survive a positive Λ to all orders. The main result is
existence, the accelerating positive-energy warp-drive solution the literature has lacked:
admissible shells exist at small acceleration about a static anchor (surface DEC for
2m/R < 24/25), and a slow fixed-axis burn assembles into one real-analytic spacetime, its
constant-u cuts characteristic (shell fixed order-by-order, not Cauchy-evolved); the
rotating axis stays open. The acceleration–compactness frontier is bracketed between a
rigorous lower bound and the closed-form ceiling. Minimum-radiation steering is the
gravitational-wave–silent Damour dipole, its velocity path a hyperbolic geodesic with
Tsiolkovsky constant 3, universal in the news-silent class: the 3 is the collimation penalty
of dipole exhaust over a floor of 1. The radiating equilibrium is linearly unstable (flux
anti-damps the radial mode), yet growth ∼ luminosity is bounded over any finite burn by
the fuel budget. The drive is causal, subluminal, and a reaction drive needing no exotic
matter in the thin-shell idealization: steering a warp drive is a problem of energy budget,
not negative energy.
1 Introduction
Alcubierre’s warp drive [1] (reviewed in [2]) showed that general relativity permits a compact
region (a “bubble”) to be transported through an ambient spacetime while its interior remains
locally flat. It required exotic matter: the construction violates the null energy condition (nec),
and with it the weak, strong, and dominant conditions. This is not specific to Alcubierre’s ansatz.
Santiago, Schuster, and Visser [3] proved that generic warp drives, defined through the standard
shift-vector ansatz, violate the nec; Lobo and Visser [4] had earlier quantified the unavoidable
exotic content, and Pfenning and Ford [5] showed that the required negative energy is further
constrained by quantum inequalities [6].
Two design approaches dominate the field, and both are overconstrained. Metric-first
designs [1, 7, 8] choose the geometry and let Einstein’s equations determine the matter, which then
turns out to be exotic. Source-first or static designs [9, 10, 11, 12] prescribe a physically reasonable
matter profile and tolerate the geometry that results; these reach positive energy or reduced exotic
content, but are static or constant-velocity. The positive-energy claims of Lentz [13] and
Fell–Heisenberg [14] have not survived scrutiny: for the zero-vorticity Lentz soliton the Eulerian
energy density integrates to zero over each slice, so it cannot be everywhere positive [3], and a
direct Eulerian-frame computation exhibits the negative regions [15] (Table 1). Bobrick and
Martire state the gap directly: warp drives “do not have any natural way of changing their
velocities,” and “no metric which describes an accelerating warp drive solution has so far been
presented in the literature” [9]. Barzegar, Buchert, and Vigneron [16] (see also [17]) raise a

1

complementary structural requirement: a physical warp drive must possess covariant (not merely
coordinate) velocity and acceleration, and a mechanism derived from matter rather than
reverse-engineered from a chosen metric. Buchert and Frackowiak [18] report an instability of warp
fields in that metric-first class (Sec. 13).
The most recent positive-energy constructions sharpen this gap rather than close it. Fuchs et
al. [10] match an explicit positive-energy shell, but at constant velocity, and name radiation- and
momentum-transfer steering as the open direction beyond simple photon rockets; Rodal [11]
reduces the exotic requirement to a predominantly positive invariant density at constant velocity,
and Garattini and Zatrimaylov [12] obtain a strictly non-negative energy density for a warp bubble
in a de Sitter universe, with the weak and null conditions holding only up to a total divergence
that averages to zero, and only when the bubble moves radially at the speed of the cosmic
expansion, a velocity fixed by the background rather than chosen by the drive. One construction
does accelerate positive-energy matter: Huey’s membrane models [19], whose sub-luminal member
satisfies the dominant energy condition on the induced surface stress-energy. Its branes, however,
are of infinite extent, a non-compactness Huey himself identifies as the chief obstacle to realism
and as an unsatisfying evasion of the no-go theorems. A non-compact wall is not an isolated
system: it carries no Bondi four-momentum, so no momentum balance constrains its acceleration.
On the propulsion side, Füzfa [20] already used the exact Kinnersley photon rocket as a model of
relativistic interstellar travel, but as a bare accelerating point mass, without an excised cavity, a
matched passenger shell, a surface energy-condition guarantee, or a kinematic frontier: we adopt
his exterior and add the passenger load it carries. The criterion the present construction meets,
and none of these do, is the observer-robust pointwise dominant energy condition, in bulk and shell
and over all observers, on a compact, asymptotically flat drive, together with a covariant,
matter-derived acceleration (Table 1, Sec. 13).
A second, structural objection is also met: the causal-control obstruction of Krasnikov [21] and
Everett and Roman [22], that an Alcubierre bubble cannot be steered from inside because its wall
lies outside the ship’s causal future, does not arise here, the shell being a timelike worldtube in
causal contact with the passenger worldline at every retarded cut (Sec. 13).
In a companion paper [23] we tested whether the source-first route can be pushed to
admissibility. Using the observer-robust energy-condition framework of Ref. [24], an exhaustive
scan of compact-support shell configurations found none admissible: the dominant-energy failures
localize at the smooth source–vacuum transition, persist in the static limit, and appear as
Hawking–Ellis [25] Type IV stress-energy in the transition tail. The obstruction is geometric, tied
to the transition itself rather than to the warp velocity, and we re-derive this boundary cost in
closed form below (Prop. 6). That result reframes the problem: if the obstruction is the transition
itself, the design should remove the smooth source–vacuum transition from the interior and
abandon the requirement that the exterior be vacuum. The simplest positive-energy replacement
for that vacuum is outgoing null radiation.
This paper. We propose a third approach, worldtube-first. We prescribe the passenger worldtube
(a timelike curve Γ together with a regular cavity) and we accept positive null radiation as the
exterior matter we are willing to carry; Einstein’s equations then determine the shell. The exterior
is taken to be the exact Kinnersley–Robinson–Trautman photon rocket [26, 27, 28, 29, 30], a known
exact solution whose source is pure outgoing null dust. Its energy conditions reduce to a single
positivity, n2 ≥ 0 (Sec. 5), and its central worldline is covariantly accelerated, by the recoil of
anisotropic radiation, exactly as a photon rocket. We excise the worldline singularity, replace
r < R(u, Ω) by a regular cavity, and match across a timelike worldtube using the standard Lanczos
junction together with flux-source balance laws [31, 32]: the timelike-shell balance laws with an
external crossing flux, mandatory here because the exterior radiation is emitted at the shell, the
cavity within being vacuum.
The resulting object, a radiative momentum warpshell, is causal, subluminal, self-gravitating,
and built entirely from matter that satisfies the energy conditions in the bulk. Its acceleration is
real and is paid for by Bondi mass loss. The organizing principle is not the metric but a
conservation law: no asymptotically flat, positive-energy drive can change its velocity without
radiating four-momentum to null infinity, and the construction below is the explicit positive-energy
object that realizes it. This formalizes, as a Bondi–Sachs conservation law, the momentum-balance
obstruction Bobrick and Martire could state only heuristically [9]: “whatever is the acceleration
mechanism, it must obey the conservation of 4-momentum. This is because all warp drive
spacetimes are asymptotically-flat.” That is exactly Theorem 1: in the asymptotically flat setting
considered here, a confined drive acquires four-momentum only by radiating it, and we exhibit the

2 What is a warp drive? A worldtube-first definition
The recurring criticism of warp spacetimes is that their “motion” is a coordinate statement [16, 17]:
a shift vector β i (t, x) in an ADM form ds2 = −dt2 + γij (dxi + β i dt)(dxj + β j dt) encodes a
coordinate velocity that need not correspond to any covariant acceleration of matter. We therefore
adopt an operational, gauge-invariant definition.
Definition 1 (Warpshell). A warpshell is a spacetime M = M− ∪Σ M+ such that: (i) the interior
M− is a regular timelike worldtube (the cavity) with bounded tidal tensor along a central
worldline Γ; (ii) Σ is a timelike hypersurface across which the induced metric is continuous (the
first junction condition) and the surface stress-energy Sab is well defined; (iii) the exterior M+
satisfies the energy conditions; and (iv) the central worldline carries a prescribed covariant proper
√
acceleration aµ = uν ∇ν uµ with gauge-invariant magnitude aµ aµ . The warpshell is admissible if,
in addition, Sab satisfies the surface energy conditions.
Definition 1 makes “acceleration” a scalar invariant of a physical worldline, not a coordinate
velocity, answering the covariance demand of Refs. [16, 17]. It also inverts the design order
demanded there: we will fix Γ and the admissible exterior matter first, and let the Einstein
equations return Sab . Alcubierre and Natário bubbles are warpshells in this sense only in a
degenerate, non-accelerating limit; the constant-velocity positive-energy shells of Refs. [9, 10]
realize (i)–(iii) but reach (iv) only in the non-accelerating limit aµ = 0. Our task is to satisfy all
four with the exterior energy conditions intact.
In what sense a warp drive. The object we construct is, mechanically, a rocket: it expends
mass as radiation, and its passengers feel proper acceleration. It differs from a bare rocket, and
belongs to the warp-drive lineage, through the structure of Definition 1: a passenger cavity that is
exactly flat, so the interior tidal tensor vanishes and the occupants sit in an accelerated laboratory,
joined to an exterior that is positive-energy in every frame. It is the accelerating, positive-energy
member of the family whose constant-velocity members are the shells of Refs. [9, 10]: it shares the
protected flat interior with the Alcubierre program and adds a covariant, matter-derived change of
state of motion. Unlike the Alcubierre bubble it carries no local expansion or contraction of space
ahead of or behind the cavity (no York-time “warp field”): the cavity is rigidly Rindler-accelerated
and the exterior is the photon rocket, so the warp-drive connection is precisely the decoupled,
tidally protected passenger laboratory, not a spatial-expansion mechanism.
A positive-energy, accelerating, compact object is not barred by the Santiago–Schuster–Visser
no-go [3]: the construction lies outside that theorem’s prescribed-shift hypotheses (Sec. 13.1).
We claim no superluminal travel and no reactionless propulsion (Sec. 14); the drive is strictly
subluminal, so the causality pathologies of superluminal warp drives [33] do not arise. The
covariant acceleration long missing from positive-energy warp drives, and the conservation law that
sets its cost, are the subject of Sec. 3 onward.
3 The no-reactionless-steering principle
The organizing principle of the paper is a statement stronger than “this particular construction
radiates”: no confined, positive-energy drive can change its velocity without radiating
four-momentum to null infinity. We state it first, as a model-independent theorem whose proof
does not use the construction, and only then build the saturating object: the explicit
worldtube-first construction of Sec. 4 below (whose control law (14) (positivity of the null-dust
amplitude, Prop. 3) and momentum balance (12) we invoke as needed) is its local, positive-energy
saturating face. We state the law, identify the one hypothesis the construction must supply, and
record how the Kinnersley photon rocket of Sec. 4 saturates it.

a Bondi–Sachs expansion with the standard Newman–Penrose peeling behavior [34, 35, 36].

Confined sources only. The theorem governs the total Bondi four-momentum of a confined
source, and the confinement hypothesis gives the no-go its force. A device that expels unconfined
massive reaction mass can change its payload’s velocity while the total Bondi four-momentum

The remaining hypothesis needing care is the identification of PBi with the kinematic steering of
the passenger worldline.
Proposition 1 (Center of momentum at null infinity). The integrated momentum change of a
confined drive between two stationary Bondi frames is the integrated ℓ = 1 flux ∆PBi of (1),
unambiguously and supertranslation-covariantly; the corresponding velocity change is read from the
Bondi-frame ratio PBi /PB0 between those frames. The instantaneous identification of a
center-of-mass worldline with the ℓ = 1 mass-aspect moment is, by contrast,
supertranslation-ambiguous in general: the relativistic center of mass has no invariant curved-space
analog, the same ambiguity that lets a deformable body “swim” through curved spacetime by cyclic
shape change with no momentum exchange [52, 53, 54]. We therefore state the instantaneous
COM↔scri identification as a hypothesis (a choice of Bondi rest frame); it is discharged exactly by
the present construction, whose exterior is the Kinnersley rocket with the closed-form worldline
PBµ (u) = m(u)v µ (u) [28], leaving no center-of-mass ambiguity. The two effects are complementary:
the no-reactionless-steering principle forbids reactionless velocity change (∆PBi ), whereas swimming
produces a finite position displacement at fixed PBµ .
Corollary 5 (Unambiguous steering velocity for the Kinnersley rocket). For the Kinnersley
photon-rocket exterior of Sec. 4 the outgoing Robinson–Trautman congruence is shear-free, so its
canonical cuts are good cuts (σ 0 = 0) on which the ℓ = 1 mass-aspect moment is
supertranslation-unambiguous [30] (a choice of this canonical shear-free cut family with the explicit
Bonnor four-momentum, not a frame-invariant center of mass), and there the Bondi
four-momentum is exactly the Bonnor value PBµ (u) = m(u) v µ (u) [28] at every retarded cut (v µ the
unit four-velocity, m(u) > 0). This closed-form four-momentum discharges the hypothesis of
Proposition 1: the instantaneous steering velocity v i (u) = PBi (u)/PB0 (u) is read directly off PBµ with
no center-of-mass choice, so the no-reactionless-steering principle applies to the instantaneous
velocity, not merely between stationary Bondi frames. The residual freedom in these good cuts is the
four-parameter BMS translation subgroup, under which PBµ is invariant (a translation aµ shifts only
the mass-dipole moment, J µν → J µν + aµ PBν − aν PBµ , not PBµ itself ); PBµ transforms as a four-vector
under the complementary Lorentz factor, which relates distinct Bondi rest frames. The steering
velocity v i = PBi /PB0 is therefore unambiguous: translation-invariant, and Lorentz-covariant between
rest frames. (We claim the unambiguous velocity, not a fully invariant center-of-mass worldline,
which the no-go does not use.)

13 Comparison with the literature
We now place the construction against the prior no-go and positive-energy literature.
13.1 Relation to the Santiago–Schuster–Visser theorem
Reference [3] proves that generic warp drives violate the nec. Its hypotheses define a warp drive
through the Natário prescribed-shift ansatz: unit lapse and flat spatial slices, with the flow encoded

26

entirely in the shift. Under those hypotheses the Eulerian observers form a zero-vorticity geodesic
congruence, and the Raychaudhuri argument forces the violation. Note what the theorem does not
assume: it explicitly tolerates Israel–Lanczos–Sen thin-shell distributional curvature and an
asymptotically Schwarzschild exterior, so a non-vacuum exterior is by itself no escape. The
radiative warpshell falls outside the hypotheses for two independent reasons. First, we do not
prescribe the metric, and the Robinson–Trautman exterior has neither unit lapse nor flat spatial
slices, so its Eulerian congruence is neither geodesic nor zero-vorticity and the Raychaudhuri step
never begins. Second, the drive radiates to I + , so it is not a compactly supported modification of
Minkowski at all. The matter we install (positive null radiation) satisfies the nec by Proposition 3.
The theorem remains valid under its own hypotheses; the present construction lies outside them.
How positivity is established matters as much as that it is. Ref. [3] shows that earlier
“positive-energy” claims inspected only the comoving Eulerian observer, whereas the energy
conditions quantify over all timelike and null observers; our bulk and shell margins are computed
observer-robustly over the boost manifold, so the binding test is not concealed by a frame choice,
the failure mode the theorem exposes. The only place the nec could fail is the shell, and
Theorem 2 establishes that it does not on an open admissible set.
13.2 Structural soundness beyond the energy conditions
Barzegar, Buchert, and collaborators [17, 16] stress that energy-condition violation is not the only
pathology of warp spacetimes: a physical drive must also have a well-defined asymptotic structure
and covariant kinematics, and prescribing a metric and reading off whatever stress-energy the field
equations return can manufacture sources with no interpretation as physical matter. The
worldtube-first construction sidesteps this by installing interpretable matter region by region (real
outgoing null dust outside, vacuum cavity, Hawking–Ellis Type I shell; Sec. 2, Prop. 3), with
velocity and acceleration the Bondi-frame components of the geometrically defined four-momentum
PBµ (u) (Cor. 5), not coordinate artifacts. The recent no-go theorems of Ref. [16] and the
realizations of Buchert and Frackowiak [18] (which also report instability, consonant with our
stability caveat of Sec. 14) bear on the restricted, metric-first (prescribed-shift) class and lie outside
the worldtube-first hypotheses. One of those theorems requires explicit comment. Barzegar,
Buchert, and Vigneron prove that R-Warp models violate the dec [16]: an R-Warp model has
vanishing ADM energy, EADM = 0, whence the positive-energy theorem forces it to be Minkowski,
so any nontrivial member must violate the dec. Their argument is correct, and it does not apply
here, because its first step rests on a hypothesis the present construction does not satisfy. An
R-Warp model is defined by the conjunction of their restrictions R1, R2, R3b, R4 (and R6); the
vanishing of EADM follows from R3b, spatial flatness, its proof being the substitution hij = δij into
the ADM surface integral. Our spatial slices are not flat: they carry the Schwarzschild-like mass
aspect of the exterior, and the drive has EADM = m > 0 strictly (Lemma 1), the positive mass
permitted for a dec-satisfying, non-Minkowski, asymptotically flat spacetime. The construction
also fails R2, the constant (unit) lapse and geodesic slicing: the exterior lapse (41) is non-constant
and the shell is not comoving with a geodesic foliation. With R3b relaxed and m > 0, the
implication EADM = 0 ⇒ Minkowski ⇒ dec-violation no longer applies, and the positive-energy
theorem instead accounts for the positivity of the mass. Two further requirements of Ref. [16] are
satisfied here: that the energy conditions of a thin shell be evaluated on the induced junction
stress-energy rather than a bulk Einstein tensor (they are, on the Lanczos tensor Sab throughout,
Lemma 10), and that a metric below C 2 be treated distributionally rather than pointwise (it is, by
the Israel formalism). Their theorem on non-vacuum spacetimes admitting a
vanishing-mean-curvature foliation is the Hamiltonian constraint read at K = 0: the constraint
then reads 16π E = (3)R − |K|2h − 2Λ, so its hypothesis (3)R < 2Λ + |K|2h is identically the statement
E < 0. Since E = Tab na nb for the unit normal n of any slice, the weak energy condition forces
E ≥ 0 for the Eulerian observers of every foliation; here it holds in the exterior (Prop. 3), trivially
in the vacuum cavity, and strictly on the shell for all x ∈ (0, 1) (Lemma 3; distributionally, on the
Lanczos tensor Sab , as Ref. [16] itself requires). The escape is thus independent of the foliation
chosen. The spacetime is free of closed causal curves on the finite maneuver slab, the Rindler
horizon staying off the cavity for λ = aR < 1; eternal acceleration is not claimed (Sec. 14).
A further structural objection to the superluminal bubbles is met here rather than avoided.
Krasnikov [21], and Everett and Roman [22], observed that the crew of an Alcubierre-type bubble
cannot causally control it: the bubble wall lies outside the causal future of the ship, so the region
that must be manipulated to steer is unreachable from inside, and the maneuver has to be
arranged in advance along the whole trajectory. Here the shell is a timelike worldtube in causal
contact with the passenger worldline Γ at every retarded cut, and the steering input, the

27

acceleration history a(u), acts on it through matter the passengers carry and modulate. The
maneuver is therefore causally controllable from the interior, which makes the optimal-control
formulation of Sec. 12 a statement about a drive rather than about a prescribed geometry. The
construction thus lies outside the structural no-go results as well as the null-energy one.
13.3 Quantum inequalities do not apply
Ford–Roman–type quantum inequalities [6, 79, 5] bound the magnitude and duration of negative
energy densities; the radiative warpshell contains none (bulk and admissible shell stress-energy are
positive pointwise), so the quantum-inequality no-go that rules out Alcubierre and Krasnikov [21] is
inapplicable, independently of the debated fundamental status of the classical energy
conditions [80].
13.4 The acceleration gap
Table 1 positions the construction. Bobrick–Martire and Fuchs et al. exhibit positive-energy shells,
but only at constant velocity. The soundness of these constant-velocity shells is itself contested:
Barzegar, Buchert, and Vigneron [16] (Error 18 and App. B) argue that the Fuchs et al. shell does
not solve the Einstein equations, the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff system not being solved in the
stated context, and attribute the same defect to Bobrick–Martire, following Santiago et al. [3]. We
take no position on that dispute. The construction below is an exact solution, a Kinnersley exterior
joined by a Lanczos junction whose balance laws hold in closed form, so the objection does not
arise here. To our knowledge, the radiative warpshell is the first compact, subluminal, matched
flat-cavity construction to accelerate by a covariant, matter-derived recoil, with the observer-robust
dominant energy condition in bulk and shell and a closed-form steering law, filling the gap those
papers identify [9, 10]. The closest membrane-based proposal is Huey [19], which likewise
accelerates from an extrinsic-curvature jump, and evaluates the energy conditions on the induced
surface stress-energy. It is the one prior construction that accelerates positive-energy matter. Huey
gives both a sub-luminal and a super-luminal model, and the sub-luminal one satisfies the dominant
energy condition, established on the junction tensor over all future-pointing causal observers; the
super-luminal one satisfies the null and weak conditions but violates the dominant condition at the
outer branes. The distinction from the present work is therefore not the energy condition but
compactness: Huey’s branes extend infinitely in the directions parallel to the wall, which he
identifies as the chief obstacle to realism and as an unsatisfying way to evade the superluminal
no-go theorems. Carrying no Bondi four-momentum, such a wall is not constrained by Theorem 1.
The present construction is compact, asymptotically flat, subluminal, matched to a flat cavity, and
satisfies the balance in the closed form −ṁ ≥ 3m|a|. Fuchs et al. [10] point to radiation- and
momentum-transfer steering (mass shed as focused emission, with ADM/Bondi
four-momentum [81] as the governing constraint) as an open direction beyond simple photon
rockets; the present work carries out that program, supplying the explicit matched positive-energy
metric, the conservation law that sets its cost, and the dominant-energy-admissible saturating
instance. The recent positive-energy constructions of Rodal and Garattini–Zatrimaylov sharpen the
same distinction (Sec. 1); the table below and its footnotes record where each sits.
13.5 What is new
The ingredients (photon rockets [26, 28, 58, 29], Israel junctions [31, 67], radiating and thin
shells [83, 84], and the Bobrick–Martire taxonomy [9]) are individually classical; the novelty is their
synthesis into the compact, accelerating, positive-energy drive of Sec. 1, set against its nearest
neighbors in Sec. 13 and Table 1. The thrust is sourced by physical radiation recoil, which
distinguishes the construction from the other classical accelerating exact solution, the
C-metric [85], whose uniform acceleration is supplied by a conical-singularity strut reaching to
infinity rather than by emitted radiation.

here: the drive is subluminal and horizon-free on the finite maneuver slab, so there is no
horizon to seed it. The angular modes of the full accelerating shell and the dec-respecting
finite-thickness wall remain open.
15 Conclusion
We have inverted the warp-drive design problem. Rather than prescribing a metric (and inheriting
exotic matter) or a static source (and forgoing acceleration), we prescribe the passenger worldtube,
accept positive null radiation as the exterior, and solve for the shell. The exterior is an exact
photon rocket whose energy conditions reduce to n2 ≥ 0, and steering obeys the positive-energy
control law −ṁ ≥ 3m|a|. Admissible accelerating shells exist at small amplitude about the static
Schwarzschild–Minkowski anchor, their matching closing by an implicit-function argument on the
round-sphere rigidity operator with the ℓ = 0 monopole absorbed by the radial breathing mode. An
acceleration–compactness frontier caps the proper acceleration, below the rigorous closed-form
ceiling 21 (1 − x).
The organizing principle is model-independent: no asymptotically flat, dominant-energy drive
with a confined material source can change its velocity without radiating four-momentum to null
infinity (Theorem 1). This flux balance is classical Bondi–Sachs; our contribution is to read it as a
no-reactionless-steering principle, the general-relativistic counterpart of the relativistic rocket, and
to read it directly from the curvature across the Kinnersley rocket, static Schwarzschild, and
Vaidya. Reactionless steering is therefore impossible, and the velocity reading is exact between
stationary Bondi frames and, for the closed-form Kinnersley rocket, instantaneous (Prop. 1). Our
construction is the explicit positive-energy instance realizing this law for an accelerating warp
drive, with the minimum-radiation maneuver the gravitational-wave-silent Damour dipole.
Acceleration is paid for by positive radiation, not negative energy. Within the compactness window
x + 2aR < 4/5 the thin shell is realized as the limit of an admissible radiating tangential-pressure
wall that stays dominant-energy-admissible under its own O(µ) back-reaction, and the worked
saturating burn (the Supplement) lies inside this window throughout, so that burn itself, not
merely its endpoints, is realized

The natural objection to an all-orders claim is that the reduced constant-u system is
over-determined in appearance (Lemma 7), so that a nonlinear source at high order might project
onto a cokernel. It cannot, for the following reason. In its unreduced form the axisymmetric first
junction is square: with intrinsic coordinates (u, ϑ, φ), the unknown functions are the exterior
shape R(u, ϑ) together with the interior embedding into the flat cavity, its cylindrical radius
ρ(u, ϑ), its axial height z(u, ϑ), and its time T (u, ϑ), and axisymmetry leaves exactly four rows, one
per unknown:
• [hφφ ] = 0 fixes ρ = R sin ϑ algebraically;
• [hϑϑ ] = 0 fixes the height z by the quadrature (70), whose one constant of integration is an
axial translation of the interior, that is the ℓ = 1 rigid-translation gauge itself;
• [huϑ ] = 0 fixes the interior clock tilt, and it is here that the shape velocity cancels;
• [huu ] = 0, stripped of that velocity, fixes the shape R itself, algebraically.
No row is left over, so the apparent over-determination of the reduced system is an artifact of the
reduction, not a property of the junction. The count alone does not prove surjectivity, a system of
differential and quadrature relations not being decided by counting functions; what removes the
cokernel is that each block is discharged by a theorem that carries none. The first two rows are the
nonlinear isometric embedding, which solves for every near-round induced metric of positive
curvature (Nirenberg–Pogorelov, with Cohn–Vossen rigidity fixing the residual rigid motion), so it
imposes no solvability condition at any amplitude; the last two are the order-independent
characteristic block, whose invertibility off the ℓ = 1 gauge is the fixed nondegenerate minor (77).
The count shows there is nothing left over for those two theorems to miss, which is why the
mechanism does not degrade with the order.
